# /automations proof: what I take (18 Oct 2026)

Drawn from what I already know of Apple's product pages (the iPhone and Vision Pro pinned scenes), awwwards Sites of the Day with smooth scroll and WebGL, linear.app and rauno.me. I did not browse them live in this session: page reads cannot show motion, and they cost tokens. Plus GreenSock's official agent skills (github.com/greensock/gsap-skills, gsap-performance and gsap-scrolltrigger, read rather than installed). Nothing copied.

1. **Apple: one object, one sentence, one scroll.** A pinned scene does one thing (the object turns, a line lights), and the copy waits until it is done. The pace comes from the space between moments. So each stage holds one object and one claim, and only 01 pins.
2. **Apple: restraint around the hero.** Everything near the product photo is quiet type on black, so the product is the only rich thing on screen. Here the signature appears twice per page and the interface stays matte, so it can shine.
3. **Awwwards (WebGL sites of the day): scroll owns the 3D.** The best ones tie the scene to scroll position with no easing lag of their own (the smooth scroll is the only smoothing), and the canvas sleeps off-screen. A second easing on top of Lenis is what makes WebGL feel "floaty".
4. **Linear: hierarchy from weight and grey, not boxes.** Mono indices, one hairline between rows, three greys of text, and nothing outlined. The switch rows, receipt and FAQ use that grammar.
5. **rauno.me: motion is short, starts fast and never overshoots.** Interface motion runs 0.25–0.45 s on an expo-out, and every state can reverse mid-flight without a jump, which is why the receipt uses transitions and rolls rather than keyframes.
